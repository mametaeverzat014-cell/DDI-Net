## How to read it

**`test_S1_fraction`** is the headline. It is the share of test pairs whose two endpoints *both* appeared in a training pair - the pairs a model can score from memorised node identity without representing the interaction mechanism at all.

**`drug_overlap_train_test`** is the count of molecules present on both sides. For `drug` and `scaffold` this is a hard invariant: the verifier raises `LeakageError` and aborts the run if it is not zero. For `random_pair` it is expected, and reported rather than rejected - the scheme exists to be measured, not used.

**`discarded_pairs`** is nonzero only for the strict schemes. They throw away pairs straddling the validation and test drug groups, because otherwise a drug tuned on during validation reappears at test time. The leaky scheme has no drug groups to straddle and so discards nothing - part of its apparent advantage is simply that it trains on more data, and that should be stated when comparing.

## What this does not show

Nothing here is a performance number. A scheme that leaks does not necessarily produce a higher AUPRC - that is the hypothesis Phase A tests, and it has to be tested by training models on these splits. This table establishes only that the schemes differ in the way they are supposed to differ, and by how much.
